## Interview questions you should expect, and the answers

**"Your R² is near zero. Isn't that bad?"**
For next-day *returns*, near-zero R² is the correct and expected result — it is
what market efficiency implies, and negative values are normal (they mean worse
than predicting the sample mean). That is why I do not report R² as a headline
for return targets. On the volatility target, where there is a real mechanism,
R² is 0.229.

**"How do you know you don't have lookahead bias?"**
Four independent guards. Per-feature causality testing corrupts all data after a
cut point and asserts nothing before it moves. Purge correctness asserts
`max(train) + horizon < min(test)` on every fold. Preprocessing is fitted
fold-locally. And the strongest one: I permute the labels and assert skill
collapses to zero — that checks the conclusion rather than one specific mistake,
so it catches leaks I didn't think to look for. It is paired with a positive
control so it can't pass by the pipeline simply being broken.

**"Why is volatility predictable when direction isn't?"**
Volatility clusters — calm follows calm, turbulence follows turbulence. It's the
ARCH effect Engle won the Nobel for, and it's mean-reverting, so both
persistence and reversion carry information. Direction doesn't cluster that way;
if it did reliably, it would be arbitraged away. The skill also decays with
horizon exactly as a clustering mechanism predicts: +14.2% at 5 days, +9.7% at
10, +0.9% at 21.

**"Why two databases?"**
Free-tier Postgres is 0.5 GB. The full universe is ~8.9M rows, about 1.5 GB with
indexes. Hot/cold tiering with on-demand promotion keeps the working set in
Postgres and the long tail in Parquet at ~120 MB, queried by DuckDB in-process.

**"Is a 1-timestep LSTM an LSTM?"**
No — it's a dense layer. With a single timestep there's no previous hidden state,
so the recurrence never fires. My first version had that bug across all six
"deep" models. I found it, wrote `make_windows` for real 60-bar lookbacks, and
added a test asserting the input timestep dimension is greater than one.

**"How did you pick the best model?"**
Not by taking the top of the leaderboard — with several models and a handful of
folds, that's selecting and reporting on the same data. `select_model_honestly`
scores on every fold except the last, and the held-out final fold gives an
unbiased estimate.

## Talking about the pivot

If asked why the project forecasts volatility rather than price:

> "I built the validation harness first, then ran it across 12 tickers and 4
> horizons. Nothing beat the naive baseline on direction — 336 evaluations, all
> negative skill. Rather than tune until something looked good, I took that as
> the real answer and went looking for a quantity that *is* predictable.
> Volatility clustering is a well-documented effect with a mechanism, and the
> harness confirmed it at 14.2% skill with significance. The null result is in
> the README because finding it is the part that took judgement."

That is a stronger answer than any positive number, because it demonstrates the
thing that's actually hard to teach: knowing when a result is real.
